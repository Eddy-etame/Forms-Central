# 01 · The teacher's three ideas, engineered

> Lens T. Full MCP (T1), hacker-proof lead data (T2), reply from the client dashboard with AI (T3), and what else the end-client can do (T4).
> It is one of five research lenses behind the rebuild plan ([`README.md`](README.md)). Written on 2026-09-28 by an independent research agent, with live web research and code references; every URL was accessed that day. **[HYPOTHESIS]** marks an unverified assumption, and "secondary" marks a third-party source.
> Court references (A1–A9, B1–B6, N1–N23, §5) point to `docs/tribunal/05-verdict.md`.

---

All URLs were accessed on 2026-09-28. "Secondary" marks figures that came from a third-party blog or a search summary rather than the vendor's own page. No files were written to the repo. The only artifact is a token-estimate script in the scratchpad (`tok.mjs`).

**Four code facts that shape every row:**
1. **MCP.** It has 4 tools, pretty-prints full payloads (`app/api/[transport]/route.ts:39,100`) and never sets origins (`:58-70`).
2. **Portal.** It is read-only, returns up to 500 rows with no paging (`app/api/portal/leads/route.ts:27-32`), and end-clients are never notified. The lead email goes to the developer's `clients.email` (`app/api/submit/[id]/route.ts:337,754-769`).
3. **Attachments.** They sit in a **public** bucket, named from a timestamp, the submitter's name and the field (`route.ts:583-622`).
4. **Plaintext copies.** Full payloads are copied in plaintext into `failures_log` (`route.ts:569,771,818`), into notification emails (`lib/email.ts:116-128`) and into webhooks (`lib/webhooks.ts:72`).

**T1 tool contract (13 tools) and token budget.**

```
# R = readOnly, I = idempotent. Output sizes are estimates at ~3.6 chars/token.
setup_site(site_url, business{name,email,lang}, framework, fields?, autoreply?, idem_key?) -> {client,form,origins,env,files?,invite}  ~150 (+~450 with file)  free  I
create_end_client(name,email,lang)        -> {client,invite}          ~40   free for 1, paid from 2  I
create_form(name, site_url or origins[])  -> {form,origins,env}       ~80   free (plan cap)          I
set_origins(form, add[], remove[])        -> {origins}                ~40   free                     I
assign_form(form, client)                 -> {ok}                     ~15   free                     I
set_autoreply(form, {fr?,en?}, enabled)   -> {ok}                     ~15   free                     I
get_embed(form, target, mode)             -> {files:[{path,content}]} ~200-450 free                  R
get_form_health(form)                     -> {origins_ok,...}         ~60   free (daily canary paid) R
list_leads(filters, fields?, limit<=50, cursor?, format rows|summary) -> {cols,rows,next}  ~60/lead, ~50 summary  free 30d / paid all  R
get_lead(id, full?)                       -> one lead, fields <= 2k chars  ~150-600  free           R
draft_reply(lead, body?, tone?)           -> {draft,preview,approve_url}   ~120  paid (queues, never sends)
list_drafts(status?)                      -> {rows}                   ~15/draft  paid                R
whoami()                                  -> {plan,quotas,scopes,checkout} ~40  free                 R
```

**Token rules behind the contract:**
- No pretty-printing.
- Errors come back as `isError` with a one-line remedy and a checkout URL (~25 tokens).
- Idempotency uses a natural key (site origin plus client email) or an explicit `idem_key`. A replay returns `{existing:1}`, so retries do not burn Free's form cap.
- `structuredContent` is used only where an output schema exists, and the text block carries the same compact JSON.
- The happy path from zero to a live form is **2 calls (`setup_site` then `get_form_health`) and ~700 tokens**. Today it is 3+ calls, ~2.5k tokens and 3 human steps, one of which (setting origins) is impossible for a self-serve user.

**How competitor MCPs compare:**
- **Tally:** 20+ tools, OAuth, free.
- **Jotform:** 5 tools, OAuth (https://tally.so/help/best-mcp-form-builders).
- **Postbox:** MCP on Free (https://usepostbox.com/pricing).
- **Agency Label:** free MCP and "ships zero AI" (https://agencylabel.com/pricing).
- **None of them** creates or assigns end-clients, returns relay files per framework, or queues replies for owner approval. That is where Inlet can differ.

## T2: Hacker-proof lead data

**Honest boundary:**
- Only T2.4 (Vault) meets "an attacker inside the server can never read leads", and only if the encrypting script is not served by the compromised server.
- T2.1–T2.3 protect database dumps, backups, leaked database keys and public file URLs. They do not stop a live compromise of the Vercel runtime.

## Top 5 from this lens

**Answer to the teacher: ship T3 first,** after a thin T2 foundation. T1 comes second, and T2's full end-to-end tier (Vault) third.

1. **T3.1 Threaded inbox with reply.** It is the only one of the three ideas a business owner sees and pays for. It turns the €10 read-only portal into a €12–20 product, and no form backend or Agency Label offers it.
2. **T2.1 with T2.2 and T2.3 (sealed at rest, private files, fewer plaintext copies; 6 days).** Build this before T3 adds new personal-data tables, so message bodies are born encrypted. Because this default tier stays readable to the server, it does not block AI.
3. **T1.1 `setup_site` with T1.3 compact reads.** Two calls and ~700 tokens take a form from zero to live. It fixes the origins bug on the agent path, saves the author an hour per client site, and is the one MCP capability competitors lack (creating and assigning end-clients).
4. **T3.2 and T3.3 AI triage and drafts (3.5 days).** This is the AI the teacher asked for, at under $0.30 per site per month. It must run on a paid provider (A4).
5. **T2.4 Vault forms.** It is the only honest answer to "an attacker in the server". Ship it last and opt-in: it removes alert content and server-side AI, so it must not be the default T3 runs on.

**Why T2 decides T3 and AI:** the tier decides where AI can run. Default forms (T2.1) keep server-side AI. Vault forms move AI to the owner's browser (Chrome's on-device APIs) or turn it off.

**Dependency order:**
1. Orders A4 and A7 (a paid AI provider; one paid email provider with inbound).
2. T2.1–T2.3.
3. T4.1 (passwordless login).
4. T3.1, then T3.2/T3.3.
5. T1.1/T1.3, then T1.4 (reuses T3.1's send path).
6. T1.6 OAuth, then T1.8 listings (needs the custom domain and B2 legal pages).
7. T2.4/T2.5 (needs passkeys from T4.1).

**Budget:** the Top 5 total about 30 developer-days. That is well beyond the tribunal's 110-hour cap, so the first 12 days should go to T2.1–T2.3 and T3.1 only.

## Ideas I rejected and why

- **Charging for MCP access itself** (today's gate at `[transport]/route.ts:159-171`). splitforms, Formcarry, Tally, Postbox and Agency Label all give it away. Charge for end-clients, replies and Vault instead.
- **One KMS key per tenant.** At $1 per key per month, it eats ~8% of a €12 site fee for no extra protection over one key-encryption key wrapping per-tenant data keys.
- **Searchable or homomorphic encryption for server-side search on Vault leads.** It is overkill: a small business's leads fit in browser memory (the portal already fetches 500 and searches client-side, `PortalLeads.tsx:37-44`).
- **AI replies that send themselves by default.** Liability, AI Act Art. 50 disclosure duties, and spammers would get answers. Only the opt-in, disclosed acknowledgment (T4.9) survives.
- **One natural-language "do_anything" MCP tool.** It saves tokens but cannot carry read-only or destructive annotations, which the Claude directory requires, and it is unverifiable.
- **Running Inlet's own inbound mail server.** MX servers do not fit serverless hosting. Resend, Brevo, Postmark or SES do it for cents.
- **A two-way WhatsApp Business API inbox first.** Meta verification, template approvals and per-message fees are heavy for one developer. The zero-cost `wa.me` handoff (T4.13) captures most of the value first.
