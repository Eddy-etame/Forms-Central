> Lens M. 33 ideas on the value metric, packaging, add-ons, reseller economics, services, alternative capture and payment rails, plus the recommended price sheet and the path to €1k / €3k / €10k a month.

Written on 2026-09-28 by an independent research agent, with live web research and code references; every URL was accessed that day. **[HYPOTHESIS]** marks an unverified assumption, and "secondary" marks a third-party source.

## Top 5 from this lens

1. **M1 + M2 + M4, the per-client price sheet.** It is the only change that triples ARPA without new infrastructure, since the per-end-client limit is already enforced in code.
2. **M16 + M5, the resale kit with margin math and the €29 founding price.** "Pay €54, bill €225–375" is the sales pitch, and 3 founding agencies at €29 pass the 12-27 gate exactly.
3. **M7, the studio retainer ladder (Engine 1).** It is the fastest money: invoiced directly, with no checkout, Paddle approval or stranger path needed.
4. **M20 → M21, the audit-to-setup ladder.** It brings cash in the first month (≈€1.3k/mo at 4 audits and 3 setups) and turns cold businesses into Engine 1 and 2 clients.
5. **M29 + M30 + M31: Paddle first, reverse trial, and moment-of-value prompts.** Engine 3 cannot exist without a checkout, and today's silent email pause is the worst possible upgrade moment.

**Rule for every add-on:** sell before you build. Each add-on needs 3 prepaid commitments before a day of code goes into it. This matches the verdict: pricing v2, WhatsApp and custom-domain portals are frozen until 12-27, but a price sheet used in sales calls is a document, not a build.

## Ideas I rejected and why

- **Charging per form.** Forms cost nothing to create, and splitforms' own pricing article puts it this way: "Run an agency with a dozen small client sites and you're paying for forms, not traffic" (splitforms.com/blog/how-much-does-a-contact-form-cost). It also punishes tidy one-form-per-page setups.
- **Charging per seat.** Agencies are 1–5 people, and seat count doesn't grow with their client count. Per-seat pricing ($25 per user at Help Scout and Front, $14–36 at Missive) is help-desk logic. Seats stay a fence, not the price.
- **An AppSumo lifetime deal (M25).** Refunds of 16–17%, 30–40% more tickets, and costs that grow with email and AI use. The buyers are freelancers who would lock in the least valuable use case. M26, a capped prepay, gets the cash without the permanent liability.
- **Sponsors or ads in the free tier's auto-replies.** They would appear in the end-client's transactional emails to its own leads. The brand and ePrivacy risk is far larger than the few euros it would bring.
- **SMS alerts at a flat price.** Twilio charges $0.0798 per SMS to France (twilio.com/en-us/sms/pricing/fr), so 250 alerts cost about $20 against a €5 price. US 10DLC adds $4.50–44 of registration plus $1.50–10 a month. Offer WhatsApp (M8), or SMS only as a pass-through.
- **Charging for storage or retention of text leads.** Storing text costs almost nothing, and GDPR pushes toward deleting it. Sell retention controls (M15) instead.
- **Per-lead pricing as the main metric** (WhatConverts charges $0.10 per lead, CallRail $0.02 per form submission). It penalises success and invites "that was spam" disputes. Use it only as overage (M31).
- **A self-host licence or open-core.** The Economist already put its expected value at ≈$455 (`02-economist.md:241`). Chain of title is unsettled (B1), and the code is not a moat.
