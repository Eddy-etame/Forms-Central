# 05 · Moonshots and moats

> Lens X. 21 bold moves, including the single bold bet: every lead answered, and proven.
> It is one of five research lenses behind the rebuild plan ([`README.md`](README.md)). Written on 2026-09-28 by an independent research agent, with live web research and code references; every URL was accessed that day. **[HYPOTHESIS]** marks an unverified assumption, and "secondary" marks a third-party source.
> Court references (A1–A9, B1–B6, N1–N23, §5) point to `docs/tribunal/05-verdict.md`.

---

**Ground rules for reading this**
- All URLs were accessed on 2026-09-28. "(secondary)" marks a vendor-review or blog source, not the vendor's own page.
- **Effort** is developer-days for one person to reach a *first paying version*. It **excludes** the court's prerequisites that every idea needs anyway: the §5(a) A-orders, a legal entity, legal pages and a checkout (`05-verdict.md` §5).
- **Context I held myself to.** The court capped the 90-day sentence at 110 h, of which about 60 h is for the resale test. The only verified user is the author's own studio (N1). Code is not a moat (`05-verdict.md` §3, "Defensibility 3/10").
- Every moat below is therefore **data, switching cost, position or regulation**, never "we wrote the code".

### The single bold bet: "Every lead answered, and proven"

Inlet stops selling "form submissions received" and sells **time-to-first-answer**, measured and proven monthly under the agency's brand. The build order is X2 → X3 → X9, then X1 and X8 only after the gate passes.

**Why it is hard to copy**
- Form backends see only the inbound.
- AI receptionists see only calls.
- GHL requires the agency to move its whole stack.
- Inlet sits on the inbound, the reply and the monthly proof, so every month adds benchmark data (X19) and knowledge cards that a competitor cannot copy in a weekend.

**It fits the court's cap.** X2 (3 d) + internal X3 measurement (1 d) + X9 v1 (3 d) = about 7 days, or 56 h, inside the court's ~60 h resale budget.

**Preconditions**
- Paid EU AI only, never the free Gemini keys (court order).
- An AI disclosure line under AI Act Art. 50.

**90-day proof test**, 2026-09-28 → 2026-12-27, on the court's own gate dates:
- **Baseline (before 2026-10-19).** For two weeks, add a one-tap "I've answered" link to the notifications on the author's client sites. This gives a pre-change median time-to-first-answer.
- **2026-10-28.** Turn on X2 on ≥3 of the author's client sites. Targets:
  - ≥50% of clean leads answered through tap-to-reply;
  - a median time-to-first-answer at least 50% below baseline;
  - zero owner-flagged factual errors in AI drafts (every draft reviewed).
- **2026-11-27.**
  - The first X9 monthly letter has gone out.
  - ≥2 of the author's client businesses have signed a separate ≥€15/site/month line (the court's gate).
  - ≥5 agencies have seen a demo using data from their own clients.
- **2026-12-27.**
  - ≥3 external agencies pay ≥€29/month (the court's gate).
  - ≥2 of them run response-time proof on ≥3 end-clients each.

**Kill criterion**

  - owners use tap-to-reply on fewer than 30% of leads over 60 days; or
  - the median time-to-first-answer does not improve by 50%.

## Ideas I rejected and why

1. **A voice AI receptionist that answers phone calls.** Inlet has no position on the phone line. The job needs numbers (porting, telecom rules) and demands voice quality one person cannot support around the clock. The price floor is already low: Rosie $49/mo (above) and GHL Voice about $0.06/min (https://netpartners.marketing/gohighlevel-voice-ai-conversation-ai-pricing-2026/, secondary). The 62% of unanswered calls is real, but it is someone else's category.
2. **Zero-knowledge forms for clinics and health.** Encryption does not exempt a host from health-data law:
   - France requires HDS certification (v2 since May 2026, EEA hosting) for anyone hosting health data for others (https://www.donneespersonnelles.fr/hebergement-donnees-sante-hds).
   - A US HIPAA route on the current stack means Supabase Team ($599/mo) plus a BAA add-on of about $350 (https://www.hipaavault.com/resources/is-supabase-hipaa-compliant/, secondary), before the first customer.
3. **Standalone whistleblowing SaaS**, as opposed to X15's agency add-on. The market prices at $1 per employee, GlobaLeaks is free, and the buyer is HR or legal. The schema's `NOT NULL` IP and fingerprint columns fight anonymity.
4. **AI site builders (Lovable, Bolt, v0) as the main channel.** Lovable Cloud already provisions its own database, auth and functions (https://lovable.dev/blog/lovable-cloud). The platform can bundle forms whenever it likes, and most of its 100k+ daily projects are prototypes (https://www.getpanto.ai/blog/lovable-statistics, secondary).
5. **Lead-to-cash (deposit or payment link inside the reply).** Payments already exist in Jotform, Tally and Cal.com's free plan (https://cal.com/blog/calendly-pricing). Stripe Connect needs an entity, and a 1% take on low-volume showcase sites is noise.
6. **Pay-per-lead pricing.** Every spam classification becomes a billing dispute, spam already counts against quotas (N3), and showcase-site volumes are too low to make per-lead revenue meaningful.
7. **Lead enrichment (Clearbit-style).** Most small-business leads are consumers, which makes enrichment a GDPR problem, and the category is commoditised inside HubSpot.
