## Verdict on the plan

1. **What holds.** The skeleton of the pivot survives:
   - own clients first, invoiced directly;
   - per-client price unit;
   - Paddle only for software;
   - AI replies sent by a human;
   - the court's nine orders as Phase 0;
   - sourced rejections of AppSumo, paid search, llms.txt and SMS.
2. **What breaks: time and order.**
   - With the unproven "AI halves it", Phases 0–2 need about 311 h, and about 545 h without it. The window has 220 h, and the court capped it at 110 h.
   - The most expensive block, a 23-day inbox, is built on a gate that never tests it. The court's named pivot trigger, the report, waits until Phase 2.
3. **What breaks: truth.**
   - The moat sentence is false: LeadDuo, Postbox, Jotform and HubSpot Free already let owners answer leads.
   - "Every lens ranked it #1" is false: only 1 lens of 5 did.
   - "Verified response time" is only a log of taps.
   - The money table fails the plan's own Gate C.
4. **What breaks: blockers.**
   - Chain of title (court order B1) has disappeared from the plan.
   - The repo is public and carries a map of live vulnerabilities.
   - Nothing measures whether business owners will log in at all.
5. **Overall score.** 38/100 as an executable plan, which counts as **7.6%** under the house calibration (38 × 0.2). The plan's own "past 90%" framing counts as 18% at most.

## What the plan got right

1. **The court's orders come first.** F1 is the nine immediate orders, at the court's own effort (3.5 d ≈ 28 h, V:273), done "before a stranger is charged" (R:42, R:79).
2. **The value metric is right.** The price is per client, and per-lead billing is used only for overage (R:25, R:203). The market supports it (AgencyAnalytics charges $20 per client, 03:15), and the code already enforces a per-end-client limit.
3. **The payment rails are right.** Engine 1 and services are invoiced directly, and Paddle bills software only (R:164, R:227). This matches Paddle's bar on "IT services … not related to a software offering" (V:118, N7; Paddle's acceptable-use page, updated 13 Apr 2026).
4. **AI stays safe by default.** A human sends every AI reply, and auto-send is parked (R:209, R:120). That avoids Art. 50(1) and liability, and F1 moves AI to a paid provider (A4).
5. **The encryption tiers are honest where it counts.** R states that the default tier does not stop an attacker inside the server, and keeps Vault opt-in because it kills server-side AI (R:57, R:62; 01:66-77).
