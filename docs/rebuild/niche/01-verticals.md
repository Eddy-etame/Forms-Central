## 0. Bottom line

1. **In almost every vertical where a lead is worth a lot, a vertical tool already bundles website lead capture, a CRM and automated follow-up.** Prices run from €0 to $300 a month:
   - gyms: Kimono €39, Ma Salle de Sport from €39, Deciplus €79–299, Gymdesk $75–200, Kicksite $49–199, PushPress Grow $329;
   - real estate: La Boîte Immo sites from €75, Modelo, Ubiflow;
   - trades: Jobber $49 and up;
   - weddings: HoneyBook $29–109, Tripleseat;
   - cars: DealerCenter, Spider VO;
   - US local businesses in general: GoHighLevel agencies at $297–597 per location.

   Inlet cannot win by building vertical features.
2. **Inlet's real slot is the same in every vertical: a companion to whatever software the business already runs.** It sells three things the vertical tools don't advertise on the pages I read:
   - a check that the website form actually works;
   - proof of how fast the first answer came, under the studio's brand;
   - a hosted request page, plus a WhatsApp tap, for businesses whose leads don't come through a custom form.
3. **Go vertical in who you call and what you say for 90 days, not in what you build.** The best wedge is **combat-sports and independent fitness gyms in French-speaking Europe**: France first, then Belgium and Switzerland, Québec later. It scores **3.85/5**. Real estate follows at 3.55, and roofing and high-ticket home improvement is the best vertical with no author advantage, at 3.40.
4. **Chance of 10 paying customers by 27 Dec:**

   | Path | Probability |
   |---|---|
   | Fight and fitness, direct, billed as the studio's "lead line" | about **30%** |
   | Real estate | about 10% |
   | Home improvement | about 10–15% |
   | 10 generic hand-coding agencies | under 5% |

   All four are my judgment. The fight-and-fitness figure is conditional on the chain-of-title letter (F0) arriving by early December, because no invoice goes out before it (`docs/rebuild/README.md:94`).

## 1. Method and weights

Each criterion is scored 1–5 for the **French-speaking market first**, which is where one person can sell within 90 days. The US picture is given in notes.

| Criterion | Weight | Why this weight |
|---|---|---|

| (b) Website-form lead volume per business | 10% | Mostly **[HYPOTHESIS]**: no public per-business data. The 14-day baseline (`README.md:180`) measures it |
| (c) Cost of a slow answer | 15% | Speed-to-lead evidence per vertical |
| (d) Gap left by incumbents (5 = nobody captures web leads well) | 20% | If the incumbents close the gap, the niche is closed |
| (e) Reachable for one person | 15% | A buyer list of known size is evidence (brief) |
| (f) Fit with Inlet's code | 10% | Forms, portal, auto-reply, sender name per tenant, canaries |
| (g) The author's unfair advantage | 15% | Live combat-sports/gym and real-estate client sites |

**A sensitivity check changes nothing at the top.** Re-weighting toward the 90-day goal (e 25%, g 25%, d 15%, a 10%, c 10%, f 10%, b 5%) keeps the same top three: combat 4.20, independent gyms 3.60, real estate 3.50. Places 4 to 10 sit within ±0.25 of each other, which is noise.

### Six findings the table forces

- **F1. "The vertical tool already does it" is the norm.** The gap for Inlet is narrow everywhere. Monitoring, first-answer proof and studio branding are the only parts no vertical tool shows on its pricing or feature page.
- **F2. The US is closed within 90 days for one person.** US gyms and martial-arts schools are served by:
   - Gymdesk ([pricing](https://gymdesk.com/pricing): "Capture leads and follow up automatically");
   - Kicksite ([pricing](https://kicksite.com/pricing/): lead forms plus free unlimited one-way texting);
   - PushPress (Free core with "unlimited leads", Grow at $329: [pricing](https://www.pushpress.com/pricing));
   - Spark at $249 ([GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/recreation-wellness-software/a/spark-membership/));
   - GoHighLevel: $297 for unlimited sub-accounts ([pricing](https://www.gohighlevel.com/pricing)), and agencies package gyms at $297–597 per location ([netpartners](https://netpartners.marketing/crm-for-gyms/)).
- **F3. The channel doesn't match the product.** Gym leads arrive by Instagram DM and WhatsApp as well as forms ([Hyperleap](https://hyperleap.ai/blog/instagram-chatbot-fitness-studios-membership)). Inlet sees only forms. The court already names a WhatsApp majority as the trigger for a different product (`05-verdict.md:400`).
- **F4. Verticals that need photos are blocked by the code today.**
   - The 6 MB body cap cannot be reached under Vercel's 4.5 MB limit (`05-verdict.md:134`).
   - Uploaded files are public until F4 (`README.md:98, 178`).
   - So roofing, cars and remodelling score f = 3 until private direct-to-storage uploads exist.
- **F5. The speed-to-lead evidence is strong only in some verticals.** Primary studies exist for real estate (WAV, NAR), legal (Clio) and cars (Pied Piper). For gyms and trades it comes from vendor blogs (Clubworx, customerflows), so the c-scores there are softer.
- **F6. Associations are a trap despite being easy to reach.** They combine free tools, budgets that volunteers vote on, and a September-only intake.

**Risks, and the mitigation for each**

3. **Fitness Vendor at €99.99 with AI.**
   - Undercut on price (€29 founding) and on proof (studio-branded report, canary).

4. **Health data.**

   - Only after A9 removes operator-readable passwords (`05-verdict.md:271`).
6. **Seasonality.** January is about 12% of sign-ups, and September matters for clubs. Sell "ready before January" with a 15 Dec cut-off.
7. **Minors.** The form collects the parent's name and contact, not the child's.

## 4. Vertical or horizontal, and the 10-in-90 answer

**Should Inlet go vertical?** Not as a product. Yes as a 90-day go-to-market.

- The code stays horizontal: no belt ranks, class booking or billing. Kimono already sells those at €39.
- What becomes vertical:
  - the calling list;
  - one template: fields, the auto-reply script and the forbidden fields;
  - the hosted page;
  - the report's wording ("trial requests, came, joined");
  - the choice of which agencies to recruit (sport-club studios, not generic hand-coders).
