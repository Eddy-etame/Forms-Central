## 0. The answer in six lines
1. **Run a two-niche pre-sale sprint from Thu 1 Oct to Wed 14 Oct 2026, and decide on Thu 15 Oct.** That is before Gate A (Mon 19 Oct, R:191) and inside the court's 28 Oct window for "10 external discovery conversations" (V:305).
2. **The instrument is a paid "Founding Pass",** not a waitlist and not an LOI:
   - **(A) studios:** €87, which is 3 × the €29 founding price (R:29);
   - **(B) clubs:** €117, which is 3 × €39.
   
   Each pass buys an audit delivered within 5 business days plus the first 3 months, credited. It is fully refundable until 30 days after the first report.
3. **Why an audit delivered now:** the money buys something that exists today. This keeps the sale lawful while chain of title (F0, R:94) is still open, and it follows Stripe's own advice on pre-orders.
4. **Niche B is sharpened:** for-profit combat-sports and fitness clubs with 1–5 locations that are **not** on an all-in-one gym CRM. Gymdesk, Spark and Deciplus already bundle lead capture.
5. **Budget: 20.5 selling hours.** A-orders keep 10 h/week of build and are untouchable.
6. **Decision rules are pre-registered** (§7). ≥3 paid deposits and ≥30% of offers → build that niche. 0 deposits from ≥6 offers → kill it. Fewer than 6 offers → the channel failed, not the demand.

## 1. What the evidence says about pre-selling

### 1.1 How much each kind of commitment counts
| Instrument | What it proves | Evidence | Weight on the scoreboard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Waitlist email | Curiosity | Vendor "benchmarks" disagree by 10× (2–5% in some, "25–85%" in https://scalemath.com/blog/what-is-a-good-waitlist-conversion-rate, with no named sources). It is not a stable metric | 0 |
| Call booked | Time | The Mom Test's commitment currencies are time, reputation and money; nice words without advancement are "zombie leads" (https://businessbookclub.substack.com/p/free-book-summary-the-mom-test-by) | Funnel metric only |
| LOI | Stated intent | "LOIs are intended to be non-binding… no legal impact on rights, obligations" (https://commonpaper.com/blog/what-is-an-loi/); see also "The Letter of Intent Fallacy" (https://medium.com/entrepreneurs-first/the-letter-of-intent-fallacy-9929c5fc3e2a) | 0.25 |
| Signed order form (price, start date, refund terms) | Contractual intent | — | 0.5 |
| Card saved with a written mandate (Stripe Checkout "setup mode") | A payment method plus consent to charge | Stripe advises pre-order sellers to "collect and save the payment method… then charge it later"; the terms must state the "timing and frequency of payments" (https://support.stripe.com/questions/accepting-payments-for-pre-orders; https://docs.stripe.com/payments/checkout/save-and-reuse) | 0.75 |
| **Paid, refundable deposit** | **Cash** | Any price above zero changes behaviour disproportionately: the "zero-price effect" (Shampanier, Mazar & Ariely, *Marketing Science* 26(6), 2007, https://web.mit.edu/ariely/www/MIT/Papers/zero.pdf) | **1.0** |

### 1.5 Rules for outreach
| Market | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **France (B2B)** | Email is allowed without consent when the offer relates to the person's profession (legitimate interest). The person must be informed and able to object simply. Each message must identify the sender and give a simple opt-out. Generic addresses (`contact@…`) of legal persons are outside these rules | https://www.cnil.fr/fr/la-prospection-commerciale-par-courrier-electronique |
| EU (all) | Data not collected from the person: the Art. 14 information duty applies (name the source, link a privacy notice) | https://gdpr-info.eu/art-14-gdpr/ |
| **US** | CAN-SPAM covers B2B. No deceptive headers or subject lines. The message must say it is an ad and carry a valid postal address (a registered PO box is accepted). ## 2. Inlet-specific constraints the sprint must respect
1. **Build hours stay protected.** The A-orders (28 h) are due 2, 5, 12 and 19 Oct (V:263-271). The sprint takes 10 h/week of selling. That leaves 10 h/week of build: 30 h by 19 Oct, which covers the A-orders.
   - Private attachments (8 h, R:180) slip to Phase 1, week 1.
   - The "I've answered" baseline (4 h, R:179) stays, because it feeds the sample report.
2. **Nothing may be sold as "Inlet".**
   - The name collides (R:332), and the public repository carries the trial.
   - Sell under the studio brand from its warm domain (R:210), as "Lead Line / Ligne Demandes" (A) and "Trial Line / Ligne Essais" (B).
   - Never link GitHub.
3. **Only claims that are true on the day.**
   - "Live on 8 sites of a multi-location sports club" is supported by V:317.
   - "Daily test request" may be claimed only once canaries run, from 14 Oct (V:313). Before that, write "from go-live".
   - No guarantee and no SLA (R:288).
   - No client named without written consent (V:368).
4. **Go-live dates.**
   - (A) needs per-agency isolation (F2) and the DPA (F3), so onboarding runs 30 Nov–18 Dec (Phase 2, R:230).
   - (B) clubs are operator-managed end-clients, so they need F3 and F4 but not F2 first. Onboarding can start 23 Nov.
   - **B's path to money is about 20 build hours shorter** (F2 is 20 h, R:201).

**Day 1, 30 minutes: answer two questions.**

| Yes | Yes | **Stripe Payment Link** (https://docs.stripe.com/payment-links/create) in EUR and USD, collecting business name and VAT ID, linked to the order terms. **Or** an invoice paid by SEPA transfer (Stripe Invoicing is ~0.4%, capped at €2, `03-money.md:59`) | 1.0 |
| Yes | No | Sell the Pass as a **studio service**: "Lead-leak audit + onboarding, credited against the subscription". If the author reads it strictly, use Checkout **setup mode** instead: card saved, mandate "first charge on onboarding day, not before 23 Nov" | 1.0, or 0.75 |
| No | — | Signed order form (bon de commande) only. Start the entity registration on Day 1: F3 needs it anyway (R:97). Stripe allows sole proprietors without a separate entity; in the US it needs an SSN or ITIN and a physical address (https://support.stripe.com/questions/selling-on-stripe-without-a-separate-business-entity; https://support.stripe.com/questions/requirements-for-having-a-us-stripe-account) | 0.5 |

**If the entity is French:**
- A deposit invoice (*facture d'acompte*) is mandatory under CGI art. 289 (https://www.legalplace.fr/guides/facture-acompte/).
- VAT on a deposit for services is due when it is received (https://www.compta-online.com/essentiel-savoir-sur-la-facture-acompte-ao2824). Under the VAT franchise, add "TVA non applicable, art. 293 B du CGI".
- Call it an *acompte* with a contractual refund clause, not *arrhes* (https://www.l-expert-comptable.com/a/532429-verser-des-arrhes-ou-un-acompte-location-saisonniere-maximum-montant-remboursement-retractation-accompte-definition-facture.html).

**Stripe's pre-order hygiene, applied:**
- partial deposits only (3 months, not 12);
- a cap of 10 passes per niche;
- cash for refunds kept aside: at most 10 × €87 + 10 × €117 = **€2,040**, left unspent until the refund windows close.

### Call script: 10 questions (past behaviour and money, never "would you buy")
1. How many client sites do you maintain, and how many have a contact or quote form?
2. What do those forms run on (your own code, CF7, Formspree, native Webflow/Framer), and how many different setups is that?
3. Tell me about the last time a client said a lead didn't arrive. How did you find out, and how long did it cost you?
4. If a form stopped sending tonight, how would you know?
5. What is in your maintenance retainer, and what does it cost per month? What did the last client who cancelled say?
6. Do you send any monthly report today? What's in it, how long does it take, and who reads it?
7. What have you paid in the last 12 months for monitoring, forms or reporting tools? How much?
8. If a one-page lead report existed for your 3 biggest clients, which client sees it first, and what must it say?

## 5. Niche B, sharpened: for-profit combat-sports and fitness clubs (1–5 locations) not on an all-in-one gym CRM

**Why B is kept, and why it is narrowed:**
- **Kept.** The author runs the sites of a multi-location club that already carries live Inlet traffic (V:317). The pain is measured (§1.4). The pool is large: 72,029 US martial-arts studios in 2025 (https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/number-of-businesses/martial-arts-studios/4187/), and 6,811 gyms in France (secondary: https://www.magicfit.fr/6-811-salles-de-sport-en-france-le-boom-qui-cache-une-crise-silencieuse/).
- **Narrowed.** Vertical software already bundles lead capture:
  - Gymdesk: embed forms, automated email and SMS follow-up, source reporting (https://gymdesk.com/features/lead-management), from about $75/month (secondary: https://www.wodify.com/blog/gymdesk-pricing);
  - Deciplus: CRM for prospects, €69–199 (secondary: https://www.skello.io/blog/logiciels-gestion-salle-sport).
  
  So clubs on those tools are **disqualified**.
- **Associations excluded.** French clubs run as associations are excluded **[HYPOTHESIS: low budgets]**. Check the site's legal notice (SAS or SARL, versus "association").
- **Why the price holds.** The average US martial-arts member pays about $100/month (Gymdesk benchmark of 4,594 gyms: https://gymdesk.com/blog/martial-arts-industry-statistics). Paris MMA runs €80–140/month (a club's own blog: https://www.nrfight.com/blog/tarif-mensuel-mma-paris). One extra member a quarter repays €39/month about 2.5× over.
- **Considered and not chosen: WordPress care-plan providers.** They are the same buyer as A, form-monitoring rivals already exist (FormTracker, FormWatch; `04-distribution.md:64`), and they bring no unfair advantage.

## 8. What the sprint can and cannot prove
**It can prove:**
- that strangers in each niche pay €87–117 *now*, at founding prices, to this founder;
- the conversations per selling hour, by channel;
- the qualified share of each pool, which gives an estimate of known size;
- the buyers' own words for the pain;
- which objection kills deals.

**It cannot prove:**
- **Retention.** A pass covers 3 months; churn shows only in months 4+.
- **List-price demand.** Founding discounts inflate conversion.
- **That owners use the buttons.** That is the Phase 0 baseline's job (R:221).
- **For A: that end-clients accept the €12–15 line.** That is second-order demand.
- **That warm channels scale.**
- **Anything precise.** 3 of 8 gives a 95% interval of **14–69%**, and 0 of 6 still allows up to **39%**.
- **Protection against a false negative.** A niche that truly closes at 30% passes BUILD only **45%** of the time with 8 offers. That is why 1–2 deposits means ITERATE, not KILL.

**Other biases:**
- Deposits from friends are softer, so they are logged apart.
- The author's charisma and his club reference will not transfer to a hire or to self-serve.
- Refunds are possible until January: count cash that is still held on 30 Jan.

## Top recommendations
1. **Run the two-niche Founding Pass sprint from 1 to 14 Oct and decide on 15 Oct.** It is the cheapest way (20.5 h, €0 of spend) to replace "demand never tested" with cash before Phase 1 spends about 56 build hours.
2. **Make the deposit a paid audit delivered now and credited to 3 months.** Money arrives now, the buyer gets something real, and it is the one lawful route while F0 is open.
3. **Settle entity and F0 status on Day 1.** They decide whether deposits are money (1.0) or paper (0.5).
4. **Size both pools by sampling 50 random entries** instead of naming 1,000. It gives Gates A and B "a reachable pool of known size" in about 3 h.
5. **Put warm channels first** (the club client's introductions, freelancers the author knows), but judge the channel on cold deposits. Cold email alone yields about 1 meeting per 60 emails.
6. **Sharpen B to clubs without an all-in-one CRM,** and disqualify on question 6. Gymdesk and Deciplus bundle the feature.
7. **Keep 10 h/week on the A-orders.** A lost lead on the live chain is a KILL criterion (V:385); a pre-sale is not worth that risk.
8. **Write the decision rules into the scoreboard before Day 1,** so the thresholds cannot be moved after the results come in.

## Rejected and why
1. **A waitlist or "join the beta" page.** Vendor benchmarks disagree by 10×, and it measures curiosity, not money.
2. **LOIs as the main metric.** They are non-binding by design; they are kept only as the fallback when there is no entity (0.25).
3. **Mystery-shopping prospects without consent.** It is deceptive, wastes their staff's time, and breaks the scanner's "never submits anything" rule (`04-distribution.md:64`). It is allowed only inside the paid audit, with the owner's prior approval.
4. **Pre-selling a lifetime deal.** 16–17% refunds, costs that never stop, and it does not test recurring willingness to pay (`03-money.md:59`).
5. **LinkedIn automation tools.** They breach the User Agreement and put at risk the author's account, which is also his client pipeline.
6. **Cold email to Germany** (UWG §7) **and to UK sole traders** (PECR).
7. **Paid ads to a landing page.** The plan already rejects paid search (R:297), and 14 days of clicks prove curiosity, not payment.
8. **Building the scanner before the sprint.** Hand scans suffice for about 16 calls; build it after the demand is shown (R:205).
9. **Real estate as niche B.** Portals and entrenched CRMs dominate **[HYPOTHESIS]**, and the author's advantage is weaker than with the club, which already has live Inlet traffic.
